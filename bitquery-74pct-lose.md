# 3 in 4 Solana memecoin buyers lose money, $269 million in 10 launch weeks 
 Pump.fun and the other Solana launchpads put out thousands of new coins every day, and each one draws a rush of buyers hoping to catch the next big one. We checked how those buyers actually did.
 G By Gaurav Agarwal · Blockchain Intelligence On-chain figures verified October 15, 2026 9 min read 

 At a glance
 In one launch week a month from November 2025 to August 2026, about 2.9 million ordinary wallets bought new Solana memecoins with SOL. Within two weeks, 74% of buyers had less SOL than they put in, after fees. The share stayed between 70% and 79% in every month. The median buyer lost $13, and together they lost about $269 million. Gains went to a few: the best 1% of buyers took 56% of all the money won, and sandwich bots trading around them came out ahead in every launch week.

## 01 · One buyer Up $4, then down $13

 On 10 June, a Solana wallet bought a new memecoin called TrumpButt for about $41 in SOL.
Half an hour later it sold the lot and was about $4 ahead.

 An hour after that, the same wallet aped back into TrumpButt with about $18. This time the price went the other way. Seven minutes later
it sold for about $1. After four trades and their fees, the wallet was down about $13.

 One coin, four trades A Solana wallet trading the memecoin TrumpButt on 10 June 2026 

 What the wallet paid (red) or got back (green) on each trade, and its running result, at the SOL price of that minute. Network fees included. 
 That wallet is close to the typical buyer we found. We followed every ordinary buyer of new Solana memecoins in one
launch week a month for 10 months. About three in four ended with less SOL than they started with, and the
middle buyer lost $13. The top 10% of buyers took 95% of what was won, and the bots that trade around new coins came out ahead.

## 02 · The coins What is a Solana memecoin launch?

 A memecoin is a token with no business behind it, only a name, a picture and a crowd. On Solana anyone can make one in a minute
on a launchpad such as Pump.fun . The new coin starts on a bonding curve, a price formula that rises as people buy.
If enough SOL comes in, the coin graduates to a normal trading pool, on Pump.fun's own exchange PumpSwap or on another exchange.
Most coins never get that far: only 1 in 58 Pump.fun coins graduates , and most
stop trading after their first day.

 Buyers are not alone at a launch. Sniper bots buy in the first second. The coin's creator, the dev, often buys in the same moment
from a bundle of their own wallets. Sandwich bots trade just before and just after a buyer to take a slice of each trade. We have
written about snipers and bundles and sandwich attacks 
before. Here we ask a simpler question. Does the ordinary buyer make money?

 From launch to graduation How a new Solana memecoin trades in its first hours 

 Steps 2 and 3 happen in seconds. The share that graduate is from our count of Pump.fun launches. 
 We counted a wallet as an ordinary buyer if it paid the network fee on its own trades and made fewer than 100 swaps on its busiest day.
It also had to put at least 0.1 SOL into new coins, about $7 to $14 over the period, and keep its coins in the same wallet. We left out each coin's dev and wallets on our lists of known bots. One person can own more than one wallet, so the counts are of wallets. Our count of real people trading on Solana 
uses similar tests.

## 03 · The count Do memecoin traders make money?

 In every launch week we checked, most buyers lost. The share ran from about seven in ten to about eight in ten,
and across all of them it was about three in four.

 Most buyers lose, month after month Ordinary buyers of new Solana memecoins who ended with less SOL, by launch week 

 One launch week a month; each buyer followed for two weeks. Unsold coins count as worth nothing. Network fees included. 
 Losing did not depend on a bad month. It held in a winter when SOL traded near $140 and in a summer when it was
below $80. Bags that buyers never sold count as worth nothing, but that changes little, because these buyers sold almost
everything they bought within two weeks.

## 04 · Other counts Why does CoinGecko say most Pump.fun traders profit?

 Other counts look kinder to traders. A study by the crypto data site CoinGecko, updated in May 2026, reported that 73% of
Pump.fun traders were in profit in April 2026. It nets each wallet's sales against its purchases across all coins in a month,
leaves out coins that were never sold, and does not filter out bots.

 Same month, different answers Share of wallets that came out ahead on Pump.fun in April 2026 

 CoinGecko figure from its report. The other two bars are from our April data, the middle one on a random sample of 1 wallet in 20. 
 We ran that method on our own April data, counting every wallet that traded on Pump.fun or PumpSwap. Only 27% came out
ahead, close to the 28% of ordinary buyers who were ahead by our own count. We could not reproduce CoinGecko's figure. Its study prices
trades in dollars, and it notes that those prices can be wrong for thinly traded tokens.

## 05 · The losses How much do memecoin buyers lose?

 Most losses were small. The median buyer lost $13, and the typical loss
was much the same in every launch week. A small group of big losers carried
much of the money. Buyers who lost $1,000 or more account for 46% of everything lost.

## 06 · The winners Who makes money on memecoins?

 The money that was won went to a few. The best 1% of ordinary buyers took 56% of all the gains,
and the next 9% took most of the rest. The other nine in ten buyers shared what was left, and most of them lost.

 A few buyers take nearly all the gains Share of all money won by ordinary memecoin buyers, 10 launch weeks 

 Buyers ranked by their result in dollars. Estimated from a random sample of 1 wallet in 20. 
 Sandwich bots were the steadiest winners. They trade just before and after a buyer to take a slice, and they made about
$13 million on the same coins in the same weeks. They were ahead in all 10 launch weeks.
Devs who buy their own coin at launch from a bundle of wallets, and sniper bots that buy in the first second, sell into the crowd
that comes after them. On coins launched from late October 2025 to June 2026, devs' bundles made at least $579 million and snipers $31.5 million .

## 07 · Timing Does buying a memecoin early help?

 Buying early helped, but only a little. Coins bought in the first minute after launch
were sold at a profit 34% of the time. Coins bought a day after launch were sold at a profit 28% of the time.

 Earlier is slightly better Coins sold at a profit, by time from launch to first buy 

 Ordinary buyers in the March, June and August launch weeks; coins bought for 0.05 SOL or more. Network fees not included per coin. 
 Even the fastest ordinary buyers lost on two coins in three. In the first minute they share the market with the sniper bots,
which buy in the same second and are built to sell first.

## 08 · Strategy Does buying more coins help?

 Spreading money across more coins did not help. Buyers of a single coin lost 71% of the time, and
buyers of many coins lost more often. Bigger stakes did a little better, but most of those buyers still lost.

## 09 · Method How we did this

 We used Bitquery's Solana trade data from November 2025 to August 2026 (the same history is sold as files on the
 Bitquery Data Store ). For each month we took one launch week and the new coins that first traded on a launchpad that week:
Pump.fun, Meteora's launch curve, Raydium LaunchLab and similar. We followed every wallet that bought one of those coins with SOL for
the launch week and the seven days after it. You can check any wallet's trades on a new coin in Claude with the Solana MCP .

 A wallet's result is the SOL it got from selling, minus the SOL it paid, minus the network fees on its own transactions. Pump.fun's
trading fee is included. If a wallet sold more coins than it bought (because it received some from elsewhere), we counted only the
part it bought. Coins not sold count as worth nothing. Priority tips and fees charged by trading terminals such as Axiom or Photon are not included,
so the real losses are a little larger.

 To find ordinary buyers we dropped each coin's dev and wallets on our lists of snipers, bundles, sandwich bots and other bots.
We also dropped wallets with 100 or more swaps on any day, wallets that never paid their own network fee, and wallets that moved coins to another wallet. Some of
the last group are wallet farms that sweep coins out of several wallets in one transaction. We measured them on a random sample of
1 wallet in 20 and scaled the counts up; on the full data the sample matched the full count to within half a point.

 We checked the work four ways. Our fee rule matched 34 trades on chain, with Pump.fun sales within 0.8%. Five wallets rebuilt
by hand from the raw trades matched exactly. The wallet in the opening matched a public Solana node. And a second set of queries,
written separately from the same rules, put the share of losing buyers within 2 points of ours for three months.

## FAQ

### Do memecoin traders make money?

 Most do not. About three in four ordinary wallets that bought new Solana memecoins ended with less SOL two weeks later, and most buyers lost in every launch week we checked. The median buyer lost $13.

### What percentage of Pump.fun traders lose money?

 About 74% of ordinary buyers in our data, and most of them in every launch week. Most of the coins came from Pump.fun.

### How much do memecoin buyers lose?

 Most lose a little; the median buyer lost $13. A few lose a lot. Together, ordinary buyers lost about $269 million in the launch weeks we followed.

### Who makes money on memecoins?

 A small group of buyers, and the bots that trade around them. The best 1% of ordinary buyers took 56% of all gains. Sandwich bots made about $13 million on the same coins.

### Why do other studies say most Pump.fun traders make a profit?

 CoinGecko reported that 73% of Pump.fun traders were in profit in April 2026, netting each wallet's sales against its purchases for the month. Run the same way on our April data, only 27% of wallets came out ahead. We could not reproduce its figure.

### Does buying a memecoin early help?

 A little. Coins bought in the first minute after launch were sold at a profit 34% of the time, against 28% for coins bought a day later. Sniper bots buy in the same first second.

 Limits on these figures
 Only trades paid in SOL are counted. Coins bought with USDC or USD1 are left out.
 Each buyer is followed for the launch week and seven days after. A coin sold later counts as unsold, and unsold coins count as worth nothing.
 Tips and trading-app fees are not counted, so losses are slightly larger than shown.
 Wallets that moved coins to another wallet are left out, because we cannot see what the coins were worth after they moved. Some are wallet farms; some are people moving coins to a second wallet.
 Launch weeks were picked to avoid days with known gaps in our data: some blocks missing in late November and early December 2025 and in April 2026, and some Pump.fun trades missing from 1 to 25 May 2026.
 September 2026 is left out because our record of token transfers ends on 30 August 2026, which we need to find coins that moved between wallets.
 Dollar figures use the median SOL price over each launch week and the week after it.
